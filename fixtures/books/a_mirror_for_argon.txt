Title: A Mirror for Argon
Author: B. Synthetic

---
# CHAPTER 1
The city of Argon had no mirrors until the glassmaker Neri built one for the mayor's daughter. The mirror was not flat. It bent every reflection two degrees toward kindness, so the daughter could bear the sight of her own grief after the river flood. Neri charged nothing for the mirror but asked the daughter to stand before it at dusk for one minute each day. The mayor thought this was superstition. The daughter thought it was the only honest hour she had.

# CHAPTER 2
When the mirror cracked, the daughter did not weep. She gathered the pieces and carried them to the river, where the ferryman refused to take cracked glass across the water. The ferryman said a broken mirror is a map with too many destinations. The daughter buried the pieces on the bank and planted salt-grass over them. In the spring, the salt-grass grew in the shape of a door.

# CHAPTER 3
The door opened only when the river ran backward. The daughter walked through it and found the same city, but every window held a mirror and every mirror showed a different person: a glassmaker, a ferryman, a mayor, a girl. She understood that each reflection had waited to be finished. Neri was there, sitting beside a cold furnace. He said the last mirror must be poured from the light of the first crack. She gave him the smallest shard from her pocket, and the furnace woke.
